## F8 · Final de vida

[Bishop et al., resumen de 2016 AAHA/IAAHPC End-of-Life Care Guidelines, PMID 27685363](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27685363/). La URL directa mostró un desafío de cookies en la consulta HTTP. Se recuperó el resumen del mismo PMID en el registro de MEDLINE de [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:27685363%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json).

> EOL care should focus on maximizing patient comfort and minimizing suffering while providing a collaborative and supportive partnership with the caregiver client.

> Veterinarians should not allow an EOL patient to succumb to a natural death without considering the option of euthanasia and ensuring that other measures to alleviate discomfort and distress are in place.

> An EOL treatment plan should consist of client education; evaluating the caregiver's needs and goals for the pet; and a collaborative, personalized, written treatment plan involving the clinical staff and client.

Respalda priorizar el confort, aliviar el sufrimiento, acordar un plan individual con el equipo veterinario y valorar con él la eutanasia.

[IAAHPC, General Practice Guidelines](https://iaahpc.org/veterinary-guidelines/general-practice-guidelines/), página pública, «The Ethical and Practical Framework» y «Pain, Suffering, and Quality of Life Assessments».

> Understand the core principles of animal hospice and palliative care, including ethical considerations, pain management, and the role of euthanasia.

> Learn to assess and manage physical and emotional suffering, emphasizing quality of life assessments and individual patient needs.

Respalda conversar sobre cuidados paliativos, alivio del dolor, calidad de vida y eutanasia según las necesidades del animal. El registro y los enlaces internos se ofrecen para preparar la conversación.
